## About

I am a senior Electronics and Telecommunications Engineering student at the Faculty of Electronics and Telecommunications, VNUHCM-University of Science. I focus on computer vision and machine learning, supported by an academic foundation in computer networks, data communications, and telecommunications.

In a seven-person Introduction to Artificial Intelligence course team, I contributed to a Vietnamese automatic license-plate recognition pipeline using YOLO, FastALPR, fast-plate-ocr, OpenCV, FFmpeg, and Python. My responsibilities included reviewing dataset organization and YOLO labels, cross-checking dataset configuration, testing multi-format FFmpeg workflows and annotated outputs, and evaluating difficult demonstration cases. The team's continuation-training checkpoint reported 99.45% precision, 99.37% recall, 99.45% mAP50, and 77.01% mAP50-95 on its validation set.

For my undergraduate thesis, I built a YOLO11 research prototype for detecting people associated with potentially dangerous objects in public-area images. I curated a dataset of 3,140 images and 10,595 labeled person instances, compared five YOLO11 variants, and selected YOLO11s after it achieved 0.87 precision, 0.69 recall, 0.77 F1, 0.80 mAP50, and 0.64 mAP50-95 on 314 held-out test images.

I value careful experimentation, measurable results, reproducible engineering, responsible use, and clear technical communication. I am seeking internship opportunities in computer vision, machine learning, computer networks, network communications, or telecommunications.

### YOLO11 Dangerous-Person Detection in Public Areas

**Date:** April 2026 to August 2026<br />
**Repository:** https://github.com/Nambekai/dangerous-person-detection-yolo11

- Built a two-class object-detection prototype with Python, PyTorch, and Ultralytics YOLO11 for human-reviewed academic analysis.
- Curated 3,140 images with 10,595 labeled person instances, including 247 self-captured public-area images and hard-negative examples.
- Trained and compared five YOLO11 variants using consistent data splits, 832-pixel inputs, and held-out evaluation.
- Selected YOLO11s based on 0.87 precision, 0.69 recall, 0.77 F1, 0.80 mAP50, and 0.64 mAP50-95 on 314 test images.
- Published training and inference scripts, YAML configurations, tests, model and dataset cards, reproducibility guidance, the thesis, and the defense presentation.
- Documented false-positive and false-negative risks, dataset limitations, privacy considerations, and the need for human oversight.
